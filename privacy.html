Privacy

Responsible

Karl Schulz and Maximilian Jager are jointly responsible for this website. Their addresses are in the legal notice. You may contact either of them about your data.

Hosting

GitHub Pages hosts this website. When you open a page, GitHub logs your IP address for security purposes (GitHub Docs). The legal basis is our legitimate interest in a secure and reliable website, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. GitHub may process these data in the United States and states that it complies with the EU‑U.S. Data Privacy Framework (GitHub General Privacy Statement).

Visitor statistics

We count page views and visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, a service of Cloudflare, Inc. Your browser loads a script from Cloudflare and sends it the page address, the referring page and page-load timings. Cloudflare Web Analytics sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device and does not fingerprint visitors. Cloudflare receives your IP address when your browser contacts its servers. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing how the simulator is used, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Cloudflare stores data mainly in the United States and the European Economic Area and states that it complies with the EU‑U.S. Data Privacy Framework (Cloudflare Privacy Policy).

The simulator

The simulator runs entirely in your browser. Your inputs stay on your device. “Copy link” writes your chosen inputs into the page address so that you can share them. The fonts are served from this website.

Email

If you write to us, we use your message and email address only to reply.

Storage period

GitHub keeps its access logs for as long as its security purposes require (GitHub General Privacy Statement). Cloudflare keeps the complete analytics records for 7 days and a reduced sample after that (Cloudflare Web Analytics FAQ). We keep emails for as long as the correspondence requires.

Your rights

Under the conditions of Arts. 15–18 and 21 GDPR, you may request access to your personal data, its correction or erasure, or the restriction of its processing, and you may object to processing based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. GitHub and Cloudflare hold the access logs and the analytics records, and you may also send requests about these data to them directly. You may also lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77).
